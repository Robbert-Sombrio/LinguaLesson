# Research: how hummingbirds fight for territory

Only facts listed here go on screen.

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|------|--------|
| 1 | Territorial hummingbirds defend food sources (flowers, feeders) because nectar fuels their whole metabolism. | Audubon, "Why Do Hummingbirds Fight So Much?" (B. Hack, 2024) |
| 2 | Threat displays include flaring the gorget (throat feathers), fanning and flashing the tail, and chattering. | Audubon (2024) |
| 3 | Escalation: plunge-dives, stabs and grabs with the bill and feet. | Audubon (2024) |
| 4 | Bouts last only a few seconds; challengers almost always back down rather than risk a full fight. | Audubon (2024) |
| 5 | Some species (e.g. Blue-throated Mountain-gem) reach heart rates of more than 1,200 beats per minute. | Audubon (2024), quoting ornithologist Scott Weidensaul |
| 6 | Adult males defend most vehemently, especially against other adult males. | Audubon (2024) |
| 7 | Anna's hummingbird dive displays reach ~385 body lengths per second (not used on screen: that number comes from courtship dives). | Clark 2009, Proc. R. Soc. B (PMC2817121); National Geographic |

On-screen claims used: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 (worded as "in some species").
