## Description
What does "ambivalence" mean, and how do you pronounce it? It's am-BIV-uh-lens, stress on the 2nd syllable, and it means feeling 2 strong things at once.

Harvard Business Review put it in a headline about AI at work: "Leaders Need to Grapple with Employee Ambivalence on AI." Excited that AI saves time. Terrified it'll replace you. That's ambivalence.

And it's easy to mix up with "ambiguous." Ambiguous is one thing with an unclear meaning. Ambivalent is two clear feelings, pulling apart.

Next time a colleague says "I have mixed feelings about this," try: "That's ambivalence. It's normal. Let's talk through both sides."
