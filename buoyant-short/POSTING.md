## LinkedIn (buoyant-linkedin.mp4 + buoyant-linkedin.srt)

**Post**
Business reporters have a better word than "good": buoyant.

Literally, buoyant means it floats. In business, it describes something rising and optimistic, and it carries a quiet extra meaning: it bounces back.

"The labour market remains buoyant despite layoffs."

That's why it's useful at work. "Sales are good this quarter" is fine. "Sales are buoyant this quarter" says they're strong and holding up.

Pronunciation: BOY-uhnt, with the stress on the first syllable. (You'll also hear BOO-yuhnt in American English.)
