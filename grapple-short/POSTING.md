**Description**
Grapple (GRAP-uhl, stress on the 1st syllable) means to struggle with a difficult problem, like wrestling it.

The Financial Times used it in a headline about Audi: "Audi to build $4bn US plant as it grapples with tariffs."

Notice the little word after it. You grapple with something.

At work, try: "We're still grappling with the budget." It's honest: it says the problem is hard, and you're working on it.

## LinkedIn (grapple-linkedin.mp4 + grapple-linkedin.srt)

**Post**
The Financial Times ran this headline recently: "Audi to build $4bn US plant as it grapples with tariffs."

Grapple means to struggle with a difficult problem. Picture 2 wrestlers locked together. Neither one has won yet, and neither one has let go.

That's why it's useful at work. "We're still grappling with the budget" is honest. It says the problem is hard, and it says you're working on it.

One detail trips people up: it's always grapple with. Grapple with the budget, grapple with a decision, grapple with tariffs.

Pronunciation: GRAP-uhl, with the stress on the first syllable.
