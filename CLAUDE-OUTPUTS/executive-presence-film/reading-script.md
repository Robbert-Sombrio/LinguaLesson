# Executive presence: reading script

Read it the way you'd say it to a client. Calm and measured, about the pace of a good podcast intro.
A blank line means a short breath. **[PAUSE]** means stop for 2 full seconds.

---

You've seen it happen. Someone walks into a meeting, says very little, and the whole room leans in.

That's executive presence. And the good news? It's built from habits. And from a vocabulary you can learn.

Here are 9 words and phrases that describe it, with real moments that show it.

**[PAUSE]**

Number 1. Gravitas.

Serious, steady weight. People with gravitas sound like they've thought about it before they open their mouth.

You'll hear it in performance reviews: "She's brilliant, but she needs more gravitas."

**[PAUSE]**

Number 2. Unflappable. Calm when things go wrong.

January 2009. Both engines fail over New York. Captain Chesley Sullenberger speaks to the cabin once: "This is the captain. Brace for impact."

He lands on the Hudson. All 155 people survive. That's unflappable.

**[PAUSE]**

Number 3. Grace under pressure.

June 2010. Steve Jobs is demoing the new iPhone 4, and the Wi-Fi in the hall collapses. He stays calm, makes a joke, asks everyone to switch off their laptops, and carries on.

The tech broke. He kept his grace.

**[PAUSE]**

Number 4. Read the room. Sense the mood before you speak.

And its partner, number 5. Strike the right tone.

In May 2020, Airbnb laid off about a quarter of its staff. Brian Chesky's letter was honest about the pain. Departing employees kept their laptops, and the company helped them find new jobs.

Even people who lost their jobs praised that letter. He read the room, and he struck the right tone.

**[PAUSE]**

Number 6. Own it. Take responsibility, fast and in public.

In 2014, Satya Nadella told a conference of women in tech that they should trust the system instead of asking for raises. Within hours, he emailed every Microsoft employee. He'd answered that question, in his words, "completely wrong."

He owned it. And the apology is what people remember.

**[PAUSE]**

Number 7. Bottom line up front. The US military calls it BLUF.

Lead with the conclusion. Then give the reasons.

Your boss has 90 seconds for your update? Start with the answer.

**[PAUSE]**

Number 8. Let it land. Say the important thing, then stop talking.

January 2007. Steve Jobs announces "an iPod, a phone, and an internet communicator." He repeats it. And again. Then he waits, until the room realizes it's one device.

**[PAUSE]**

The silence did the work.

**[PAUSE]**

Number 9. Measured. Careful, controlled, never rushed.

A measured answer sounds like this: "Let me look at the numbers and come back to you by Thursday."

It buys you time. And it sounds senior.

**[PAUSE]**

So. Gravitas. Unflappable. Grace under pressure. Read the room. Strike the right tone. Own it. Bottom line up front. Let it land. Measured.

Pick one this week. Use it in a meeting, or spot someone who has it.

Executive presence is learnable. Start today.
