# LinguaLesson voiceover script (short version, with screen directions)

**This is the editing map.** Read from `lingualesson-voiceover-READ.md` when you record. The words are the same.

**Length:** about 530 words plus 2 fill-ins, roughly 3 minutes 50 seconds at a slow, clear pace.
**Demo article:** BBC Future, *'Think outside the bots': How to stop AI from turning your brain to mush* (6 May 2026)
**Not shown on screen:** installing the extension and getting an OpenAI key. Section 2 mentions the key in one line.

- `[SCREEN: ...]` is what's on screen.
- `[SAY: ...]` marks the 2 spots where you fill in something from your real lesson.
- `(beat)` is a 1-second pause.

---

## 1. Open (0:00–0:32)

[SCREEN: the BBC article in Chrome. Click the LinguaLesson logo, hit Generate, and the lesson appears.]

This BBC article says AI might be making us **worse** at thinking.
So I'm going to use AI to turn it into an English lesson.
Yes. I see the irony.
(beat)
Under a minute later, I've got vocabulary with pronunciation, a grammar point, a quiz, and questions worth arguing about.
I'm Sean. I coach English to professionals.
And I built LinguaLesson because my students' best lessons always come from real articles.
Here's how to use it.

## 2. Getting started (0:32–1:25)

[SCREEN: click the logo and the side panel opens next to the article. Then the level picker, "Article detected", pasting a link, and typing a topic to show the 3 angles.]

LinguaLesson is a Chrome extension.
To use it, you'll need your own OpenAI key. It takes a few minutes to set up, and each lesson costs a fraction of a cent.
Once that's done, click the logo, and the panel opens on the **side** of your screen.
So your article stays right there, next to your lesson.
Choose your level: B2, C1 or C2.
Be honest here.
Too high, and the lesson feels like a **wall**. Too low, and it feels like a nap.
Then there are 3 ways to start.
Open the panel on an article, and it says "Article detected."
Or paste a link.
Or type any topic you like, pick one of 3 angles, and it writes a short article at your level.

## 3. Inside the lesson (1:25–2:40)

[SCREEN: slow scroll through the summary and vocabulary, then zoom in on one pronunciation line. Then the grammar point and the quiz. Stop on the discussion questions, and hold on the question you read.]

Here's what you get.
A short summary.
Then 6 vocabulary words, each with a definition, an example, and the pronunciation broken into syllables.
The capital letters show you where the stress goes. Listen.
[SAY: one word from your lesson, slowly]
Stress matters more than people think.
Get it wrong, and people say "sorry?" even when your grammar is perfect.
Then there's a grammar point, and a 4-question quiz to check you understood.
And then my favourite part: 3 discussion questions.
Here's one from this lesson.
[SAY: read one discussion question from your lesson]
There's no right answer. That's the point.
Pause the video, and answer it **out loud** for a full minute. Then press play.
(beat)
[SCREEN: cut back to the BBC article. Show the Adam Green quote as a lower third, credited to BBC Future.]
One honest note.
LinguaLesson builds the lesson around the article. It doesn't copy the text in.
So read the real article first.
A neuroscientist in the BBC piece says using AI to skip the thinking is like going to the gym and having a robot lift the barbell for you.
You get nothing.

## 4. Word Bank (2:40–3:07)

[SCREEN: a vocabulary exercise. Get one word wrong on purpose, then open the Word Bank.]

Now the part that actually changes your English.
After the lesson, you do quick vocabulary exercises.
Get a word wrong, and it goes straight into your Word Bank.
Then LinguaLesson brings it back later, right when you're about to **forget** it.
That's spaced repetition.
You don't learn a word by seeing it once. You learn it by almost forgetting it, 3 or 4 times.

## 5. Save, export, Teacher Mode (3:07–3:22)

[SCREEN: the bookmark icon, History, Download PDF, then a fast scroll through Teacher Mode.]

You can save your lessons, find them again in History, or export a PDF to print.
And if you're a teacher, there's a Teacher Mode that builds a full 13-section speaking lesson from one article.

## 6. Close (3:22–3:55)

[SCREEN: back to the BBC article with the panel open, then the end card with The Fluent Edge cover.]

So here's your plan for this week.
Pick 1 article you actually care about.
Read it the hard way.
Then let LinguaLesson test you, answer the questions out loud, and let the Word Bank keep what you missed.
20 minutes, 3 times a week. You'll hear the difference in your next meeting.
(beat)
And if you want more real-world English, listen to The Fluent Edge, my podcast with Dr. Howie Jacobson.
The link's below.
See you there.

## Recording notes

1. One take per section, with 3 seconds of silence before and after.
2. If you stumble, pause and restart the whole sentence.
3. Use a small, soft room, with the mic about a hand's width away.
4. Read slower than feels natural.
5. Send WAV if you can. A high-quality M4A or MP3 is fine.
