# LinguaLesson walkthrough video (The Fluent Edge)

**Format:** Walter Johnson's tool-stack style, flipped. His chapters follow the order he built things. Yours follow the order a student uses the app.
**Audience:** B2–C2 professionals learning English.
**Length:** about 8.5 minutes (roughly 1,300 spoken words).
**Demo article:** BBC Future, *'Think outside the bots': How to stop AI from turning your brain to mush* by Thomas Germain (6 May 2026)
https://www.bbc.com/future/article/20260505-how-to-use-ai-without-turning-your-brain-to-mush

Why this article: it's about using AI without letting it do your thinking. That's the exact question a smart student will ask about an AI lesson tool, so the article gives the whole video its point of view.

Square brackets are notes for you, not lines to say.

## 2. Full script

### 0:00 Cold open

[Screen: the BBC article open in Chrome. You click the LinguaLesson icon. The side panel opens and you hit Generate.]

This is a BBC article about how AI might be making us worse at thinking.

And I'm about to use AI to turn it into an English lesson. I see the irony.

[Lesson appears.]

[X] seconds. Vocabulary with pronunciation, a grammar point, a quiz and discussion questions. I'm going to show you exactly how it works, and also how to use it so it actually makes your English better.

[Time the generation on camera and say the real number. In my testing of the code, a teacher lesson takes 20–30 seconds; a student lesson is shorter.]

### 0:25 Why I built it

I'm Sean. I coach English to professionals: lawyers, engineers, doctors, people running teams in their second language.

And I don't code. I still couldn't write you a line of JavaScript.

But I've seen the same thing for years. My students' best sessions never come from a textbook. They come from a real article. Something from The Economist, The Atlantic or BBC Future that they actually want to argue about.

The problem is that turning an article into a lesson takes time. Pulling out the vocabulary, writing questions, finding the grammar. So I built a tool that does the first draft of that work for you, and leaves the thinking to you.

That's LinguaLesson.

[Optional: one real sentence here about the moment you decided to build it. A real story is worth more than anything I could write.]

### 1:10 Install it

It's a Chrome extension, so step 1 is the Chrome Web Store. The link's in the description.

Click "Add to Chrome," then click the little puzzle piece in your toolbar and pin LinguaLesson so it's always there.

When you click the icon, it opens as a side panel. So your article stays on the left, and your lesson sits on the right. You never leave the page.

### 1:40 Get your OpenAI key

OK, this is the only annoying part. You do it once.

LinguaLesson uses OpenAI to build your lessons, so you need your own OpenAI key. Think of it like a password that lets the app use OpenAI on your account.

Here's how.

Go to platform.openai.com. There's a link right inside the app. Create an account or log in.

Go to Billing and add a little credit. [Show the current minimum on screen; when I checked it was $5.]

And here's the good news. LinguaLesson uses one of OpenAI's cheapest models. A lesson costs a fraction of a cent. That $5 is going to last you a very long time.

[After you record a few lessons, check your OpenAI usage page and say the real cost per lesson here. Real numbers sell this.]

Now go to API keys and click "Create new secret key." Call it LinguaLesson so you remember what it's for.

Copy it right away. OpenAI only shows it to you once. If you lose it, no big deal, just make a new one.

Go back to LinguaLesson, paste it in, and you're done.

One more tip: in your OpenAI billing settings, set a monthly limit. Then you know exactly what you'll spend.

And your key stays in your browser. It doesn't get sent to me or anyone else.

### 3:00 The news key (optional)

There's a second box for something called NewsAPI. This one's free, up to 100 searches a day, and it lets you browse trending news inside the app.

If you're only going to paste in articles you find yourself, skip it. You can add it later.

Hit "Save Keys." Setup's over. That took about 3 minutes and you never have to do it again.

### 3:25 Pick your level

Next, choose your level: B2, C1 or C2.

Be honest here. If you pick C1 when you're really B2, the lesson will feel like a wall. If you pick B2 when you're C1, it'll feel like a nap.

### 3:45 3 ways to start a lesson

There are 3 ways in.

**1: You're already reading something.** Open the side panel and LinguaLesson notices you're on an article. It says "Article detected." One click.

**2: You paste a link.** Someone sends you an article on WhatsApp? Copy the link, paste it in, done.

**3: You browse.** If you added that news key, you can browse trending news by topic: Business and Finance, Technology, Culture and Lifestyle, Personal Development.

Or type any topic you want. "Remote work." "Electric cars." "Why Germans love paperwork." It gives you 3 different angles to choose from, and then writes a short article for you at your level.

### 4:45 Inside the lesson

So let's open the one we made from the BBC piece.

[Screen: scroll the lesson slowly. Read the real words the app gave you.]

At the top you get a short summary of the topic at your level.

Then 6 vocabulary words. Each one has a definition, an example sentence, and the pronunciation broken into syllables, with the stressed syllable in capitals.

[Pick one word from your actual lesson and say it slowly, stressing the capital syllable.]

This is the part I'd slow down on. Getting the stress wrong is one of the main reasons people say "sorry, what?" in meetings. And nobody tells you, because it feels rude.

Then there's a grammar point taken from the topic, with an example.

Then a 4-question comprehension quiz.

And then my favourite part: 3 discussion questions. These are open questions with no right answer. Say your answers out loud. Record yourself on your phone. Or bring them to your next session with me and we'll argue about them.

Now, here's something I want to be upfront about. LinguaLesson builds the lesson around the article. It doesn't copy the article in. So read the real thing.

And the BBC article itself explains why. One neuroscientist in it, Adam Green from Georgetown, says using AI to skip the thinking is "like you're at the gym and a robot lifts the barbell for you. You get nothing."

So read the article first. Struggle a bit. Then let the lesson test you. As the article says, friction is the point.

### 6:15 Your Word Bank

This is my favourite feature, and it's the one that actually changes your English.

After the lesson there are vocabulary exercises: fill in the blank, choose the right word, type the missing word.

Get one wrong and LinguaLesson saves it to your Word Bank automatically.

Then it brings that word back later, just as you're about to forget it. That's called spaced repetition. It's how memory actually works. You don't learn a word by seeing it once. You learn it by almost forgetting it 3 or 4 times.

And when nothing is due, it tells you so. It literally says your brain needs time before the next review. I love that.

### 7:00 Save it, export it

Tap the bookmark to save a lesson. Your recent lessons show up in History, so you can go back to them.

And you can export any lesson as a PDF or a Markdown file. Print it, write on it, bring it to class.

### 7:25 Teacher Mode (20 seconds)

Quick one for the teachers watching.

There's a Teacher Mode. Give it an article, pick 1 of 3 angles, and it builds a full 13-section speaking lesson: warm-up, reading, vocabulary, a grammar point, discussion, dialogues, a writing task. Then you download it, ready to teach.

Students, if you've got a coach, send them this video.

### 7:50 Close

So here's how I'd use LinguaLesson this week.

Find 1 article you actually care about. Read it yourself, slowly, the hard way. Then open LinguaLesson, do the quiz, answer the discussion questions out loud, and let the Word Bank keep the words you missed.

That's maybe 20 minutes. Do that 3 times a week and you'll notice the difference in your next meeting.

And if you want more of this, real English from the real world, come and listen to The Fluent Edge. It's the podcast I host with Dr. Howie Jacobson about leadership, mindset and how to communicate like the person you are in your first language. The link's below.

Try LinguaLesson, tell me which article you used in the comments, and I'll see you there.
