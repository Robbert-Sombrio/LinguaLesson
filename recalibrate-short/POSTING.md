Alternative: Recalibrate vs change: 1 word, different signal

**Description**
Recalibrate (re-CAL-ih-brate, stress on the 2nd syllable) is a stronger choice than "change" when you're adjusting a plan at work.

"We need to change our approach after Q3" sounds reactive. "We need to recalibrate our approach after Q3" sounds deliberate: you've looked at the data and you're making a precise adjustment.

## LinkedIn (recalibrate-linkedin.mp4 + recalibrate-linkedin.srt)

**Post**
One word I'd change in a lot of the emails I see from senior professionals: "change."

"We need to change our approach after Q3" is clear. But it can read as reactive, as if something went wrong and you're scrambling.

"We need to recalibrate our approach after Q3" says something different. You have the numbers, you've looked at them, and you're making a precise adjustment.

Same plan. Different signal to the people reading it.

Pronunciation, since it trips people up: re-CAL-ih-brate, with the stress on the second syllable.

Try it in your next update email and see how it lands.
